# Appendix: The Wider Technique Landscape

The course covers the techniques a thin film scientist reaches for most often, but the full landscape is far larger. This appendix serves two purposes: it is the suggested topic list for the [final project](../outline.md), and it is a map of what else exists, organized by the question each technique answers. Techniques marked with an asterisk are popular final project choices; anything on this page is fair game, as is any technique not listed here, proposed with a sentence of justification.

## Electronic structure

- **ARPES*** (angle-resolved photoemission): the band structure E(k) of a surface or 2D film, measured directly. The defining spectroscopy of quantum materials.
- **UPS**: valence bands and work function in the laboratory.
- **Inverse photoemission / two-photon photoemission**: the unoccupied states.
- **Cathodoluminescence***: light emission under the electron beam, mapping band gaps and defects at SEM/STEM resolution.

## Atomic structure and diffraction

- **Surface X-ray diffraction**: full surface crystallography at synchrotrons, including buried interfaces no electron can reach.
- **X-ray absorption spectroscopy (EXAFS/XANES)***: local coordination and oxidation state, element by element, crystalline or not.
- **Pair distribution function analysis***: total-scattering structure of amorphous and nanocrystalline films.
- **Low-energy electron microscopy (LEEM/PEEM)***: real-time full-field imaging of surface dynamics, growth, and phase transitions at nanometer resolution.
- **Field ion microscopy**: the ancestor of atom probe; atomic imaging of tips.
- **Neutron diffraction and reflectometry***: isotopic and magnetic contrast in depth profiles.

## Composition and chemistry

- **Nuclear reaction analysis (NRA)**: isotope-specific depth profiling, the standard for hydrogen via the 15N method.
- **Particle-induced X-ray emission (PIXE)**: trace element mapping with MeV protons.
- **X-ray fluorescence (XRF/TXRF)***: fast, standards-capable composition and areal density; TXRF monitors wafer contamination at 10^10 atoms/cm2.
- **Glow discharge spectroscopies (GD-OES/GDMS)***: rapid depth profiling through many micrometers, coatings-industry standard.
- **Electron probe microanalysis (EPMA/WDS)**: the quantitative standard for micron-scale composition.
- **Raman variants (TERS, SERS)***: near-field and plasmon-enhanced chemical mapping down to nanometer scales.
- **Sum-frequency generation spectroscopy***: vibrational spectroscopy that is interface-selective by symmetry, for buried and liquid interfaces.

## Adsorption, kinetics, and surface energetics

- **Temperature-programmed desorption (TPD)***: binding energies and coverages of adsorbates, the workhorse of surface chemistry and catalysis.
- **Quartz crystal microbalance (QCM)***: sub-monolayer mass sensing, in vacuum or liquid.
- **Contact angle and adsorption isotherm methods**: surface energy and porosity of films.

## Morphology, mechanics, and properties

- **Optical profilometry and interferometry***: fast areal topography over millimeters, complementing AFM's small field.
- **Nanoindentation***: hardness and modulus of films, with the substrate-effect corrections that make thin film indentation its own discipline.
- **Wafer curvature (Stoney) stress measurement***: in situ film stress during growth.
- **He ion microscopy***: sub-nanometer imaging of uncoated insulators, plus nanofabrication.
- **Electron holography***: quantitative electrostatic and magnetic field mapping in the TEM.
- **4D-STEM methods (ptychography, orientation and strain mapping)***: computational diffraction imaging beyond what we cover in Module 6.
- **Scanning SQUID and NV-center magnetometry***: quantitative nanoscale magnetic field imaging.
- **In situ and operando TEM***: films observed during heating, biasing, and electrochemistry.
- **Cryo-EM and cryo-FIB for materials***: beam-sensitive and hydrated films, methods imported from structural biology.
